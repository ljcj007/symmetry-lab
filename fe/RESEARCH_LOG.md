# Fe: three C4 branch-order reversals from new plane-wave calculations

The endpoint observation now has a real DFT follow-up. New Quantum ESPRESSO
calculations along the correct Cartesian-z Gamma–H line show three reversals
in the order of bands 18 and 19 with different C4 eigenvalues. All three are
present for independently converged SCF calculations on 4×4×4, 8×8×8 and
12×12×12 grids. Their locations depend on the SCF grid. The result supports the
numerical branch-order mechanism; the small local search intervals are not
physical convergence error bars.

The complete machine-readable record is
[NUMERICAL_CLOSURE.json](reports/NUMERICAL_CLOSURE.json), with raw-file bindings
in [RAW_BINDINGS.json](reports/RAW_BINDINGS.json). Original output, charge
density, XML, pseudopotentials and wavefunctions remain under `experiments/`.
No Phase 1–12 source file or website file was changed.

## Main evidence

The published eight-point data were generated by QE 7.3.1. Using the same
120 Ry cutoff, 4×4×4 SCF grid, structure, fully relativistic pseudopotential,
SOC and smearing, the new QE 7.5 calculation reproduces all 32 energies at all
eight points to a maximum absolute difference of **2.51×10⁻⁷ eV**, without an
energy shift. Direct spinor reconstruction gives the same lowest-18 C4 counts:
Gamma `(4,4,5,5)` and H_z `(4,5,4,5)` in the phase order `(7,1,3,5)π/4`.

Each density then supplied an independently calculated 101-point path. Every
refinement point was another NSCF solve using that same archived density;
no interpolation was used to generate an eigenvalue or wavefunction.

Here q(t)=(t,t,t), with q in primitive reciprocal coordinates. A phase label p
denotes the C4 eigenvalue exp(i p π/4). For each interval, the stored signed
quantity `E_b−E_a` is positive on its left and negative on its right. This
tests the exchange of the two branches, rather than just searching for a
small nonnegative band gap.

The 4³ intervals have signed endpoint gaps, in eV:

- Phase 5→1: `(+3.7390912e-5, -1.6851788e-5)`.
- Phase 1→5: `(+2.4763390e-5, -2.8221585e-5)`.
- Phase 3→1: `(+1.2269280e-5, -3.9931618e-5)`.

The complete corresponding numbers for 8³ and 12³ are in the closure JSON.
All 18 retained interval endpoints were recomputed with the NSCF threshold
tightened from 10⁻¹⁰ to 10⁻¹² Ry. Every sign reversal survived. The largest
change across all 32 endpoint energies was below **3.81×10⁻¹¹ eV**.

The lowest-18 projector remains marked `UNRESOLVED_SMALL_OR_CLOSED_OUTER_GAP`
at these near-crossing endpoints because its 1 meV guard is unchanged.
Refinement uses the separately resolved C4 labels of complete energy clusters.
A mixed degenerate cluster receives combined multiplicities and no arbitrary
ordering of its vectors. The cluster grouping threshold also remains unchanged.

## Sampling convergence and physical scope

The largest eight-point energy change is 0.39442135 eV between the 4³ and 8³
SCF grids, and 0.04573323 eV between 8³ and 12³. The SCF magnetization along z
is approximately −1.9380, −2.2611 and −2.2576 Bohr magnetons, respectively.
The three types of branch exchange persist, but their positions are not
converged to the 10⁻⁵ scale of the local searches. In particular, the third
crossing shifts by about −0.00550 in t between 8³ and 12³.

The 8³ and 12³ calculations retain their explicit failure against the original
0.05 eV *source-reproduction* gate. They are executed as separate SCF-mesh
studies; their comparison status was never relabelled as reproduction. No
threshold was loosened to obtain agreement.

The crossings belong to an exploratory lowest-18 spectral prefix, at energies
roughly 20–25 eV above the calculation's Fermi energy. They are not evidence
for an occupied-band topological invariant or a Fermi-level material effect.

At each final interval the endpoint G sets agree, with 1773, 1761 and 1765
plane waves for the three crossings. Their raw G-set hashes are retained.
Endpoint agreement alone does not establish a constant basis throughout the
interval. An independent review additionally checks the quadratic plane-wave
kinetic cutoff inequalities inside each interval; its floating-point margins
are recorded separately and are not rigorous interval enclosures.

## Execution and provenance

There are **29 completed QE runs**: 3 SCF and 26 NSCF runs, comprising **500
NSCF k-point evaluations**. The count includes reused endpoints, the different
SCF grids, the 17-point pilot, and the stricter-threshold repeats. Summed QE
stage wall time is about 363 seconds on four OpenMP/MKL threads per run;
download time is excluded. All stages ended with `JOB DONE.` and exit code 0.

The original SCF potential was unavailable. The 4³ calculation is a new SCF
whose spectrum and endpoint representations closely reproduce the published
fixture. Within each new profile, SCF density hashes bind every NSCF path and
refinement point to the same density. Different profiles are never mixed in
one crossing bracket.

The portable Windows QE asset was pinned to its published release and whole
SHA256. The first transfer stopped early at 119,846,673 bytes; its digest failed
and the file was retained without extraction. A range transfer obtained all
400,430,691 bytes and matched the publisher's whole-asset SHA256 before any
binary was executed. No system installation, WSL setup, or global PATH change
was made. `SOFTWARE.md` and `software/` retain the full acquisition record.

The startup probe saw the QE 7.5 banner and then exited on deliberately empty
input; that probe is not counted as a successful DFT run. The subsequent SCF
and NSCF logs provide the actual successful-execution evidence.

## Validation and corrections retained during implementation

The final suite has 19 passing tests, including original-source endpoint
reproduction, a known crossing, degenerate-gauge mixing, false spin lifts,
missing plane-wave weight, incompatible energy mixing, invalid energies,
changed baseline values, and density/raw-file provenance failures. Full output
is in `reports/TESTS.txt` and `reports/TEST_REPORT.json`.

Windows QE copies the original UPF with CRLF line endings. The first strict
byte check therefore rejected its postprocessing. The corrected rule accepts
only CRLF→LF normalization and preserves both raw and normalized hashes;
content edits and even appended whitespace are rejected by tests. Original
and solver-written files were not rewritten.

Independent review identified two validation gaps in the first refinement
helper: cluster labels also need a passing row-level energy-covariance test,
and the initial path must be bound to the supplied SCF density and execution
record. Both are now enforced, with counterexamples. Every actual calculation
already used the correct density and had small covariance residuals; the
frozen raw outputs are replayed under the corrected checks rather than being
silently regenerated. Review material documents the early-code reconstruction
and the changed checks.

This experiment provides finite-precision DFT branch-order evidence and its
reproduction record. A continuous symmetry-preserving underlying Hamiltonian
with these branch assignments must have a crossing between the opposite-order
endpoints. The work does not supply rigorous DFT spectral-error bounds, an
exact real-material root, or a proof that the three observed reversals exhaust
all possible crossings.
