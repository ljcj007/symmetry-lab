# Why the diagonal mirror is not an integral relabelling of a coordinate mirror

For the new model, the primitive lattice action is

\[
M_d=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its fixed-momentum condition on the reciprocal torus is \(q_x-q_y\in\mathbb Z\). This locus is one connected two-torus. By comparison, a coordinate mirror \(M_c=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1)\) fixes \(2q_x\in\mathbb Z\), giving two disconnected two-tori.

This distinction survives any unimodular primitive-lattice change. If \(M_d=P^{-1}M_cP\) with \(P\in GL(3,\mathbb Z)\), then \(M_d-I=P^{-1}(M_c-I)P\). Left and right multiplication by unimodular integer matrices preserve the ideal generated by the entries. For \(M_d-I\) that ideal is \(\mathbb Z\), whereas for \(M_c-I\) it is \(2\mathbb Z\). Hence no such \(P\) exists. Equivalently, their sole nonzero Smith factors are 1 and 2.

An ordinary real rotation can align the diagonal mirror's normal with an axis, but it changes the primitive reciprocal lattice and the fundamental torus identifications. A non-unimodular supercell transformation must explicitly track its index; it cannot be treated as a harmless relabelling of the old Cartesian torus.

The new full grey group has four representatives, versus sixteen in the earlier spinful glide model. It supplies a distinct crystallographic torus problem and a coupled-coordinate stabilizer arrangement. Its rank-two carrier is an analytic spin model; it is not a new material calculation or evidence of a topologically nontrivial bundle. Its three nonidentity period matrices arise from an explicitly certified global change of frame.
